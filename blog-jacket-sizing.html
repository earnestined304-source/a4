How to Size a Heritage Jacket Correctly

Heritage-style jackets, particularly raw denim ones, often follow different sizing conventions than typical modern clothing, and understanding these specific differences helps you order the right size the first time.

Heritage cuts often run more fitted than modern casual wear

Many heritage-style denim and canvas jackets are cut closer to the body than contemporary casual jacket styles, reflecting older workwear silhouettes rather than modern relaxed fits. Someone accustomed to a looser modern jacket size may need to size up from their usual number to get a comparable fit in a heritage cut.

Shrinkage needs to be factored in for unsanforized fabric

Unsanforized raw denim shrinks noticeably with its first wash, sometimes by a meaningful percentage in both length and width. Checking whether a specific jacket's denim is sanforized or unsanforized, and sizing accordingly if it's the latter, prevents ending up with a jacket that fits well new but too small after washing.

Layering expectations should inform sizing decisions

A denim or canvas jacket meant to be worn over a t-shirt fits differently than one meant to layer over a sweater, and it's worth deciding how you'll primarily wear a jacket before choosing a size, rather than assuming one size works equally well across very different layering scenarios.

Shoulder fit matters more than overall size label

For structured jackets particularly, shoulder seam placement affects overall fit and comfort more than the general size label does. A shoulder seam that sits well beyond your natural shoulder point, or noticeably before it, indicates an incorrect size regardless of what the label says.

Sleeve length conventions vary by heritage brand

Different heritage jacket makers follow somewhat different sleeve length conventions rooted in different historical workwear traditions, so checking actual sleeve measurements against your arm length is more reliable than assuming consistent sleeve proportions across different heritage brands.

Break-in stretch affects fit over the jacket's early life

Raw denim and stiff waxed canvas both loosen somewhat as they break in through wear, meaning a jacket that feels slightly snug when new may fit more comfortably after some wear. This is worth factoring into initial sizing decisions, since sizing up to compensate for expected snugness can result in a jacket that's too loose once broken in.

Reading a brand's specific size chart matters more than usual

Because heritage sizing conventions vary more between makers than typical modern casual clothing does, checking a specific brand's actual measurement chart, rather than assuming your general jacket size applies, is particularly important in this category.

Trying on in person remains the most reliable method

Given how many variables affect heritage jacket fit — cut philosophy, shrinkage, break-in stretch — trying a jacket on in person, ideally with the layers you'd typically wear underneath, gives a far more reliable read than ordering based on general size assumptions alone.

Between-size decisions should consider your priorities

If choosing between two sizes, consider whether you prioritize a closer fit now (accepting some initial snugness that eases with break-in) or a looser immediate fit (accepting it may become slightly loose once fully broken in). Neither choice is wrong — it depends on your own fit preferences.

The takeaway

Sizing a heritage jacket well means accounting for closer historical cuts, potential shrinkage in unsanforized fabric, and the natural loosening that comes with break-in, rather than applying typical modern casual sizing assumptions. Checking brand-specific measurements and trying on in person when possible remain the most reliable ways to get this right.
