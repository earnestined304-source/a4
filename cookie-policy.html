Cookie Policy

Last updated: September 5, 2026

What Cookies Are

Cookies are small text files placed on your device when you visit a website. They're used broadly across the web to help sites function properly, remember certain preferences, and understand how visitors generally use a site. This policy explains specifically how Jacket Heritage uses them.

Types of Cookies We Use

This site uses a limited set of cookies tied to Google's tagging service (gtag.js), which helps us understand aggregate site traffic — for example, roughly how many visitors view a given page, or which pages tend to be viewed together in a single session. We do not use cookies to build individual visitor profiles, and we do not use cookies to serve targeted third-party advertising on this site or elsewhere.

Why We Use These Cookies

Understanding aggregate traffic patterns helps us make basic decisions about the site itself — for instance, noticing whether our Journal content or product pages get more attention helps inform what we write about or how we organize the site going forward. This is the only purpose these cookies serve for us.

Third-Party Cookies

Because we use Google's tagging service, some cookie data is processed by Google as part of that service, subject to Google's own privacy practices. We do not control how Google's own systems process this aggregate data beyond what we've described here, and we encourage you to review Google's privacy documentation if you have further questions about their specific practices.

Managing Your Cookie Preferences

Most web browsers allow you to view, manage, block, or delete cookies through their own settings menus. You can typically choose to block all cookies, block only third-party cookies, or be notified whenever a cookie is set. Because this site's core content and functionality don't depend on cookies, blocking them should not meaningfully affect your ability to browse this website, view our products, or read our Journal.

Do Not Track Signals

Some browsers offer a "Do Not Track" setting. Because there isn't yet a consistent industry standard for how websites should respond to this signal, this site does not currently change its behavior in response to Do Not Track browser signals specifically, though our overall cookie use remains limited as described above regardless.

Updates to This Policy

We may update this Cookie Policy occasionally if our use of cookies or analytics tools changes. The date at the top of this page reflects the most recent revision.
